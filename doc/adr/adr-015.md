# ADR-015 — Inter-persona consultation via spawn

**Status**: Accepted
**Date**: 2026-05-05
**Author**: Aurele

## Context

The H2A protocol routes all inter-persona exchanges through the orchestrator (invariant 3 — Isolation, see `protocol/h2a.md`). Two mechanisms exist today:

- **Sessions** — synchronous interaction between the orchestrator and one persona
- **Artifacts** — asynchronous deposits in the shared space (notes, reviews, features)

When a persona in session needs an expert opinion outside their scope (e.g., archi → R&D, dev → archi, archi → terrain), the existing path is the escalation pattern (`canvas/workflows/note-escalation.md`): the persona deposits a note, the orchestrator opens a session with the recipient. This works but is heavy when the needed input is short and self-contained — it forces a full session boot for the recipient.

A first empirical test (2026-05-05) explored a lighter mechanism: the emitter persona deposits a note, a sub-agent of the recipient persona is spawned with minimal context for a one-shot reply, the orchestrator arbitrates frictions afterwards. The test produced a substantive R&D opinion (Delphi anchoring, two new risks identified, experimental program proposed) and revealed the dispositif's own structural issues through self-application.

Reference: `methodes/shared/notes/note-aurele-spawn-inter-persona-solene.md` (arbitrated note carrying frictions, resolutions, complementary actions, opportunity rule, first auto-evaluation).

The mechanism conflicts with invariant 3 if read literally: an emitter persona spawning a recipient sub-agent crosses the persona boundary without the orchestrator. This ADR formalizes the dispositif and resolves the apparent conflict.

## Decision

Adopt the **consultation** mechanism as a third exchange mode in H2A, alongside sessions and artifacts.

A consultation is a short one-shot session of a recipient persona, **proposed** by an emitter persona and **authorized** by the orchestrator before execution. The emitter persona never crosses the persona boundary unilaterally — it requests authorization, the orchestrator grants or denies, then the emitter executes the spawn. Invariant 3 is preserved: the orchestrator remains the sole boundary-crosser, even when the technical execution is delegated to the emitter.

### Constraints

| Constraint | Rule |
|------------|------|
| Recursion | Depth 1 — the recipient sub-agent MUST NOT spawn another consultation. If it needs further expertise, it signals it in its short summary's `Open` section. |
| Context | Minimal — the sub-agent receives only its persona file, its context file, and the consultation note. No emitter session history, no other artifacts. |
| Web search | Authorized — the sub-agent MAY perform web searches to verify a reference or check a fact. This relaxes the "context minimal" rule for external sources only. |
| Short summary | The recipient persona MUST produce a short session summary in `{recipient-space}/sessions/{YYYY-MM-DD}-{HHmm}-{persona}-spawn.md` carrying a `trigger:` field referencing the consultation note. |
| Continuity | The short summary MUST contain a line in `## Open` instructing the recipient to read the arbitrated note at the next real session — without this, the consultation outcome is invisible to the recipient on reboot. |

### Opportunity rule

A consultation SHOULD be triggered only when the orchestrator's arbitration requires expertise outside their direct field. Typical cases: dev → archi, archi → R&D, archi → terrain. Consultations on subjects within the emitter's own field are wasteful (cost without added value).

This rule emerged empirically from the test (the first consultation was on an architecture subject; the orchestrator's auto-evaluation noted "I would have decided the same without the spawn" — though the consultation still produced material the orchestrator would not have generated alone).

### Frictions and resolution

A consultation reply MAY carry frictions in the standard H2A format. Resolution tags are filled by the orchestrator afterwards, directly in the reply note (consistent with `exchange.md` §Artifact resolution).

No global `pxp_tag` at the note level — resolutions remain at the friction granularity. A note containing both `→ ratified` and `→ rejected` cannot meaningfully be summarized by a single tag, and aggregation rules (max, mode) lose information.

## Rationale

### Theoretical anchoring: Delphi method

The closest prior pattern is the Delphi method (Dalkey & Helmer 1963, Linstone & Turoff 1975): parallel consultation of experts without inter-expert communication, with a human facilitator synthesizing. Depth 1 + orchestrator arbitration matches the Delphi structure precisely. Delphi was designed to avoid groupthink, which the consultation dispositif also seeks to avoid.

Distinctions from related patterns:
- **Multi-agent debate** (Du et al. 2023, Liang 2023, Chan 2024): agents are homogeneous, convergence is the objective. Consultation keeps personas heterogeneous, expects divergence as input quality.
- **LLM-as-judge** (Zheng et al. 2023): one LLM evaluates another's output. Consultation is *consultation* (peer expertise), not *evaluation* — biases documented for LLM-as-judge (positional, length, self-preference) do not apply symmetrically.
- **Ensemble methods**: aggregation of statistically independent models. Consultation does not aggregate — it preserves distinct positions for arbitration.

### Why authorization, not autonomous spawn

Three options were considered for the conflict with invariant 3:

| Option | Description | Why rejected / accepted |
|--------|-------------|------------------------|
| (a) Documented violation | The dispositif explicitly derogates from invariant 3 | Rejected — weakens the invariant for the rest of the protocol |
| (b) Composed operation under authorization | Persona proposes, orchestrator authorizes, technical execution delegated | **Accepted** — preserves the invariant in spirit (orchestrator remains sole crosser) while permitting the lighter mechanism |
| (c) Invariant revision | Amend invariant 3 to allow protocol-sanctioned crossings | Rejected — too ambitious, risks cascading inconsistencies |

Option (b) reflects what actually happened in the test: the orchestrator was in the conversation at every step, authorizing each transition. The "auto" in "auto-spawn" was misleading — the technical capacity to spawn without intervention should not dictate the protocol semantics.

### Experimental tracking

For the next 3-5 consultations, the orchestrator MUST add a one-line auto-evaluation at the bottom of the reply note: *"would I have decided the same without the spawn?"* → yes / no / uncertain. After 3-5 entries, evaluate whether to keep, adjust, or abandon the dispositif. Without this trace, the value-added question remains undatared at 6 months.

### Structural limitations (added to `protocol/h2a.md` §Structural limitations)

| Limitation | Nature | Mitigation |
|------------|--------|-----------|
| Context-minimal not technically enforced | A sub-agent prompt instructs which files to read; nothing prevents reading more. | Prescriptive convention; trust in persona discipline. |
| Depth-1 not technically enforced | A spawned sub-agent could spawn another. | Prescriptive convention in the prompt. |
| Emitter capture | The emitter formulates the question; alternative framings are not visible to the recipient. | Orchestrator MAY augment the consultation note before authorization. |
| Persona performativity | A short-spawned persona may over-perform its persona compared to a long real session. | Comparative audit (post-tracking). |

### No breaking change

Existing instances continue to operate — the consultation mechanism is opt-in. The escalation-by-note pattern remains valid for cases where a full session of the recipient is preferable.
